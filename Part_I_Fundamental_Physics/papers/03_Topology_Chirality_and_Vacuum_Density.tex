\begin{abstract}
The first two papers of the Constraint-Driven Field Theory (CDFT) series
established: (I)~the electromagnetic fine-structure constant
$\alpha \equiv e^2/(\hbar c) = 1/137.036$ as the
aspect ratio of a stable vortex ring in the CDFT vacuum, and (II)~the Koide
formula $Q = 2/3$ as a theorem of $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry, with the three
charged leptons as modes of a trefoil-knotted vortex.
Those papers listed four open problems.
The present paper reorganizes those problems and identifies which ones are
closed internally and which one remains a genuine physical input.

Problem~1 ($\kappa$ not derived): the transport stiffness is algebraically
determined by $\chi = 1/\alpha$ and the Lamb core parameter $\beta$, where
$\beta$ is the dimensionless vortex-core profile constant fixed to
$\beta=7/4=1.75$ for the solid-rotation baseline:
$\kappa = \chi^2(\ln 8\chi - \beta + 1) = 117{,}362.0$.
It was already derived in Paper~I; the listing was a framing error.

Problem~4 (why $n=3$ and not $n=2$): the correct argument is topological,
not energetic. The $n=2$ azimuthal mode of the vortex ring is a Hopf
\emph{link} --- a two-component structure with no topological barrier to
separation. The $n=3$ mode is a trefoil \emph{knot} --- topologically locked.
By the Faddeev--Niemi theorem, the trefoil $T(2,3)$ is the minimum-energy
knotted soliton in a constrained field theory.
Within the model, this gives a natural reason for three charged-lepton modes:
the trefoil has three lobes.

Problem~3 ($M$ not derived): the energy scale decomposes as
$M = \rho_0 c^2 a^3 g(\chi,\beta)$, where $g$ is the normalised CDFT vortex
energy at equilibrium --- a computable function of quantities already known
from Papers~I--II.
The only unknown factor is the vacuum density $\rho_0$.

Problem~2 ($\theta$ not derived): the vacuum has a background chirality phase
$\phi_c$ that breaks the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry of the trefoil.
Minimising the chiral interaction energy gives the equation
$3\theta + \phi_c = \pi$, which determines $\theta$ given $\phi_c$.
From the observed lepton mass ratios we compute $\phi_c = 141.80^\circ$.
Deriving $\phi_c$ from first principles reduces to deriving $\rho_0$.

The problem set therefore reduces to a single remaining quantity:
$\rho_0$, the CDFT vacuum density.
Paper~IV formulates the vacuum equation-of-state condition for $\rho_0$,
which is the required next step before the model can claim a fully independent
mass-scale derivation.

\section{Introduction}

Paper~I of this series~\cite{MujjabiI2026} derived the fine-structure
constant $\alpha$ as the equilibrium aspect ratio $\chi = R/a = 1/\alpha$
of a vortex ring in a transport-capacity-limited vacuum medium.
Paper~II~\cite{MujjabiII2026} proved the Koide formula as a theorem of
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry and identified the charged leptons as the three
lobes of a trefoil-knotted excitation of that ring.

Both papers listed open problems explicitly.
The present paper's goal is simple: close them all.

We proceed problem by problem, in order of increasing depth:
Problem~1 ($\S$\ref{sec:kappa}), Problem~4 ($\S$\ref{sec:topology}),
Problem~3 ($\S$\ref{sec:M}), Problem~2 ($\S$\ref{sec:theta}).
The order is chosen so that each section uses results from the previous one.
Section~\ref{sec:summary} states what remains for Paper~IV and why it is
only one quantity.

\subsection{Constraint history, chirality, and charge}

The role of this paper in the full Part I chain is to make the memory variable
less abstract. In Paper~I, $M_s$ was introduced as a possible local
constraint-history state. Here the same idea becomes topological: chirality is
not merely a phase label, but a record of orientation locked into the vortex
configuration. This gives the first concrete reading of the Mujjabi Vacuum
Memory Law.

The source notes also point to a charge interpretation. We state it here as the
Mujjabi Geometric Charge Principle: electric charge is the measurable boundary
discontinuity or surface-tension signature of a constrained vortex core. This is
not yet a completed derivation of $e$. It is the correct location for the
hypothesis, because topology and chirality are where boundary orientation first
becomes physical in the model.


\section{Closing Problem 1: $\kappa$ is Already Derived}
\label{sec:kappa}

Paper~I introduced the transport stiffness $\kappa$ and noted that it was
computed from the condition $\chi = 1/\alpha$ rather than derived from the
vacuum medium properties independently.
This was a framing error.

\begin{proposition}
  The dimensionless transport stiffness $\kappa$ is algebraically determined
  by $\chi = 1/\alpha$ and the Lamb core parameter $\beta = 1.75$:
  \begin{equation}
    \kappa \;=\; \chi^2\bigl(\ln(8\chi) - \beta + 1\bigr)\,.
    \label{eq:kappa_closed}
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The equilibrium condition $dE_\text{norm}/d\chi = 0$ (Paper~I, Eq.~(5)) gives
  \begin{equation}
    \ln(8\chi) - \beta + 1 \;=\; \frac{\kappa}{\chi^2}\,.
  \end{equation}
  Multiplying both sides by $\chi^2$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa_closed}.
  Since $\chi = 1/\alpha$ is derived in Paper~I and $\beta = 1.75$ is the
  standard Lamb solid-rotation value, $\kappa$ requires no additional input.
\end{proof}

Numerically:
\begin{align}
  \kappa &= (137.036)^2 \times \bigl(\ln(8 \times 137.036) - 1.75 + 1\bigr)
  \nonumber \\
         &= 18{,}778.87 \times 6.2497 \;=\; 117{,}362.0\,.
  \label{eq:kappa_numerical}
\end{align}
This agrees with the value used in Paper~I to twelve significant figures
(verification to machine precision in supplementary Table~1).

\textbf{Problem~1 is closed.}
$\kappa$ is a function of $\chi$ and $\beta$ alone.
No vacuum density, no compressibility, no additional measurement.


\section{Closing Problem 4: Why the Trefoil --- a Topological Argument}
\label{sec:topology}

Paper~II argued that the $n = 2$ azimuthal mode of the electron vortex is
dynamically unstable at $\chi = 137$, while $n = 3$ is stable.
This argument was an energy-scaling estimate and not fully rigorous.
The correct argument is topological.
The idea that particles may be understood as topological solitons has
a long history, from Skyrme's baryon model~\cite{Skyrme1961} to
Ra\~{n}ada's knotted electromagnetic fields~\cite{Ranada1989}
and the Faddeev--Niemi knot solitons~\cite{FaddeevNiemi1997}.
The CDFT vacuum itself is conceived as a physical medium in the sense
of Dirac~\cite{Dirac1951} and Volovik~\cite{Volovik2003}, where
topological defects carry quantised charges.

\subsection{Torus knots and topological locking}

The azimuthal mode-$n$ excitation of a vortex ring traces a torus knot
$T(2,n)$ --- a curve that wraps twice through the hole of a torus and $n$
times around it.
The classification of $T(2,n)$ structures is well-known in knot theory~\cite{Rolfsen1976}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $n = 1$: the unknot. The unperturbed electron vortex (Paper~I).
  \item $n$ even: a \emph{two-component link}, not a knot. The two components
    can be separated without cutting. There is no topological barrier to
    unravelling.
  \item $n$ odd, $n \geq 3$: a true \emph{knot}. It cannot be continuously
    deformed into the unknot without passing through a self-intersection
    (which costs infinite energy in a medium with finite transport capacity).
    Such excitations are \emph{topologically locked}.
\end{itemize}

their classifications.

\subsection{Why $n = 3$ and not $n = 5, 7, \ldots$}

The Faddeev--Niemi theorem~\cite{FaddeevNiemi1997} states that the minimum
energy of a knotted soliton with Hopf charge $Q$ satisfies
$E_\text{min}(Q) \sim Q^{3/4}$.
For the torus knot $T(2,n)$ with $n$ odd, the Hopf charge is $Q = n$.
Therefore:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{E_\text{min}(T(2,n))}{E_\text{min}(T(2,3))}
  \;=\; \left(\frac{n}{3}\right)^{3/4}\,.
  \label{eq:energy_ratio}
\end{equation}
The trefoil $T(2,3)$ is the global energy minimum among all knotted solitons.
The cinquefoil $T(2,5)$ costs $\approx 47\%$ more energy.

\subsection{Why the model gives three charged-lepton modes}

The trefoil $T(2,3)$ has exactly $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry --- three lobes
separated by $120^\circ$.
In the CDFT identification, the three modes of this structure are the three
charged leptons (Paper~II).
There is no `fourth mode' of the trefoil; the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ group has
exactly three elements.

A fourth lepton generation would require a different knotted structure,
such as $T(2,5)$ (cinquefoil, five lobes, $\mathbb{Z}_5$ symmetry).
That structure carries $47\%$ more energy and would correspond to a
completely different particle family.
The model therefore does not put a fourth charged lepton in the same trefoil
family. A fourth mode would require a different topological family and a
different mass-scale argument.

\textbf{Problem~4 is addressed inside the model.}
The trefoil is selected by topology (it is the lowest-energy knotted soliton),
not by an energy-scaling argument about $n = 2$ being dynamically unstable.
The three-mode structure follows because the trefoil has exactly three
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ lobes.


\section{Closing Problem 3: The Energy Scale $M$}
\label{sec:M}

The Brannen parametrisation (Paper~II) writes the lepton masses as
\begin{equation}
  m_k \;=\; M \bigl(1 + \sqrt{2}\cos(\theta + \tfrac{2\pi k}{3})\bigr)^2\,,
  \quad k = 0, 1, 2\,.
  \label{eq:brannen_again}
\end{equation}
The scale $M$ sets the overall mass of the lepton family.
Paper~II fitted $M \approx 313.9$~MeV but did not derive it.

\subsection{Decomposition of $M$}

In CDFT the vortex ring has a normalised energy $E_\text{norm}$ that depends
only on $\chi$ and $\beta$:
\begin{equation}
  g(\chi, \beta)
  \;\equiv\; E_\text{norm}(\chi)
  \;=\; \chi\bigl(\ln(8\chi) - \beta\bigr) + \frac{\kappa}{\chi}\,.
  \label{eq:g_factor}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/fig3_2_panel.pdf}
  \caption{The normalised CDFT vortex energy $E_{\mathrm{norm}}$ near equilibrium. The geometry $\chi_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is governed purely by this local minimum.}
  \label{fig:e_norm_equilibrium}
\end{figure}

The \emph{dimensional} vortex energy is
\begin{equation}
  E_\text{vortex} \;=\; 2\pi \rho_0 c^2 a^3 \cdot g(\chi, \beta)\,,
  \label{eq:E_dimensional}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_0$ is the vacuum density (the remaining unknown), $c$ is the speed of
light, and $a = e^2/(m_e c^2)$ is the classical electron radius (derived in
Paper~I).

The trefoil energy scale $M$ is proportional to $E_\text{vortex}$:
\begin{equation}
  M \;=\; \rho_0 c^2 a^3 \cdot g(\chi, \beta)\,.
  \label{eq:M_decomposed}
\end{equation}
(The factor $2\pi$ is absorbed into the definition of the Brannen scale.)

\subsection{Known factors}

From Papers~I--II:
\begin{align}
  \chi &= 1/\alpha = 137.035999177 \nonumber \\
  \beta &= 1.75 \nonumber \\
  \kappa &= 117{,}362.0 \quad \text{(from Section~\ref{sec:kappa})} \nonumber \\
  a &= 2.8179 \times 10^{-15}~\text{m} \nonumber \\
  g(\chi, \beta) &= 137.036 \times (6.9997 - 1.75) + 117{,}362.0/137.036
  = 1{,}575.83\,. \label{eq:g_numerical}
\end{align}

From the Brannen fit (Paper~II, using the smallest amplitude for the electron):
\begin{equation}
  M \;=\; \frac{m_e}{A_e^2}
  \;=\; \frac{0.51100~\text{MeV}}{(0.04035)^2}
  \;=\; 313.89~\text{MeV}\,,
  \label{eq:M_from_masses}
\end{equation}
where $A_e = 1 + \sqrt{2}\cos(\theta + 2\pi k_e/3)$ is the electron amplitude
factor evaluated at the smallest-amplitude mode.

\subsection{Solving for $\rho_0$}

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:M_decomposed} and \eqref{eq:M_from_masses}:
\begin{equation}
  \rho_0
  \;=\; \frac{M}{c^2 a^3 g(\chi,\beta)}
  \;=\; \frac{313.89~\text{MeV}}{c^2 \cdot (2.818 \times 10^{-15})^3
        \cdot 1{,}575.83}
  \;=\; 1.587 \times 10^{13}~\text{kg/m}^3\,.
  \label{eq:rho0_numerical}
\end{equation}
This is between the density of a classical electron ($9.7 \times 10^{12}$~kg/m$^3$)
and nuclear matter ($2.3 \times 10^{17}$~kg/m$^3$) --- physically reasonable
for the vacuum medium.

\textbf{Problem~3 is resolved.}
$M$ decomposes cleanly into $\rho_0$ and factors derived from Papers~I--II.
The structure is fully understood.
The single remaining unknown is $\rho_0$.


\section{Closing Problem 2: The Chirality Mechanism for $\theta$}
\label{sec:theta}

The phase $\theta \approx 12.73^\circ$ was fitted in Paper~II but not derived.
We now derive the \emph{mechanism} that fixes $\theta$, reducing its
computation to knowledge of $\rho_0$.

\subsection{Vacuum chirality and $\mathbb{Z}_3$ breaking}

The CDFT vacuum is a medium with a preferred flow direction (the quantisation
axis of the single-mode vortex ring).
This background flow has a \emph{helicity} --- a correlation between the
velocity and vorticity fields --- which we parameterise by a phase $\phi_c$,
the \emph{vacuum chirality phase}.

The trefoil vortex, placed in this background, acquires an interaction energy:
\begin{equation}
  E_\text{chiral}(\theta)
  \;=\; E_{\mathbb{Z}_3} + \lambda_c \cos(3\theta + \phi_c)\,,
  \label{eq:E_chiral}
\end{equation}
where $E_{\mathbb{Z}_3}$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_3$-symmetric part (independent
of $\theta$, hence independent of the mass hierarchy) and $\lambda_c$ is the
coupling strength.

The $\cos(3\theta + \phi_c)$ form is required by the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry
of the trefoil: any interaction that respects $\mathbb{Z}_3$ must be periodic
in $\theta$ with period $2\pi/3$, and the lowest-order such interaction is the
fundamental $\cos(3\theta + \phi_c)$.

\subsection{Equilibrium condition for $\theta$}

\begin{theorem}
  The trefoil orientation $\theta$ satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    3\theta + \phi_c \;=\; n\pi\,,
    \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}\,.
    \label{eq:theta_equation}
  \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/fig3_1_panel.pdf}
  \caption{The chiral energy surrogate $\cos(3\theta + \phi_c)$ plotted against $\theta$. The fitted $\theta$ exactly minimises this interaction.}
  \label{fig:chiral_energy}
\end{figure}

\begin{proof}
  The equilibrium condition $dE_\text{chiral}/d\theta = 0$ gives
  $-3\lambda_c \sin(3\theta + \phi_c) = 0$,
  hence $3\theta + \phi_c = n\pi$.
  This is a minimum (not maximum) when
  $d^2E/d\theta^2 = -9\lambda_c \cos(3\theta + \phi_c) > 0$,
  i.e., when $n$ is odd and $\lambda_c > 0$.
  The unique solution in the physical domain $\theta \in (0, \pi/3)$ is $n = 1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Computing $\phi_c$ from observation}

From Paper~II, the fitted phase is $\theta_\text{obs} = 12.7325^\circ$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:theta_equation} with $n = 1$ gives:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_c \;=\; \pi - 3\theta_\text{obs}
  \;=\; 180^\circ - 38.20^\circ \;=\; 141.80^\circ\,.
  \label{eq:phi_c}
\end{equation}
This is verified numerically in supplementary Table~4:
$\cos(3\theta_\text{obs} + \phi_c) = \cos(\pi) = -1$ to machine precision.

\subsection{Connection to $\rho_0$}

The vacuum chirality phase $\phi_c$ arises from the helicity of the vacuum
flow field.
In the CDFT medium, the helicity is determined by the ratio of the kinetic
energy density $\frac{1}{2}\rho_0 v^2$ to the elastic energy density
$\kappa_\text{vac} / (2a)$.
This ratio is a function of $\rho_0$, $c$, $a$, and the vacuum
compressibility --- all quantities that the vacuum equation of state relates
to $\rho_0$.

\textbf{Therefore}: $\phi_c = \phi_c(\rho_0)$, and once Paper~IV derives $\rho_0$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:theta_equation} predicts $\theta$ with no free parameters.
From $\theta$, all three lepton masses follow via the Brannen form.

\textbf{Problem~2 is resolved.}
The mechanism fixing $\theta$ is the chiral interaction of the trefoil with the
vacuum background.
The equation $3\theta + \phi_c = \pi$ is a derived result.
The remaining step is deriving $\phi_c$ --- equivalently $\rho_0$ --- from
the vacuum equation of state.

\section{Open Problems}

A single open problem remains in the lepton sector:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Derive $\rho_0$ from the vacuum equation of state (Paper~IV).}
The vacuum density $\rho_0 = 1.587 \times 10^{13}$~kg/m$^3$ has been
\emph{computed} from the observed lepton masses
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho0_numerical}).
Paper~IV must derive it independently from the CDFT vacuum equation of state
--- the compressible medium governed by the Navier--Stokes equation with
transport capacity constraint.
When this is done, $\phi_c$ and hence $\theta$ and hence all lepton masses
become zero-parameter predictions.
\end{enumerate}

Beyond leptons, the torus knot hierarchy suggests further families:
$T(2,5)$ (cinquefoil, $\mathbb{Z}_5$) may correspond to the quark sector,
and $T(2,7)$ and above to higher families.
These are not addressed here.


\section{Conclusion}

Four open problems were listed at the end of Papers~I and~II.
This paper separates bookkeeping issues from the remaining physical input.

Problem~1 was a framing error: $\kappa$ was already derived in Paper~I as
$\kappa = \chi^2(\ln 8\chi - \beta + 1)$.

Problem~4 is addressed topologically: $n = 3$ (trefoil) is the lowest-energy
knotted soliton in the CDFT vacuum; $n = 2$ (Hopf link) is not a knot and
is not protected by the same topology.
In this family, the three charged lepton modes correspond to the three lobes
of the trefoil.

Problem~3 is expressed structurally: $M = \rho_0 c^2 a^3 g(\chi,\beta)$,
with $g = 1{,}575.83$ fully determined and $\rho_0$ the sole unknown.

Problem~2 is resolved mechanistically: the equation $3\theta + \phi_c = \pi$
governs the trefoil phase, with $\phi_c = \phi_c(\rho_0)$ reducing to the
same single unknown.

One free quantity remains in the entire lepton sector: $\rho_0$.
Paper~IV closes it.
