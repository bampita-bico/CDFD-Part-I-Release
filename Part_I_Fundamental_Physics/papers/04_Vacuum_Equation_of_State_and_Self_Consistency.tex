\begin{abstract}
The first three papers of the Constraint-Driven Field Theory (CDFT) series
connected the fine-structure constant to a vortex aspect ratio, proved the
Koide relation as a theorem of Z-three mass structure, and identified the
charged leptons with modes of a trefoil-knotted vortex. Within that model, the
remaining unknown is the vacuum medium density.

This paper formulates the next step by deriving the master self-consistency
condition that the vacuum density must satisfy. The condition combines the
energy-scale relation and chirality equilibrium developed in Paper III with
the Brannen mass parametrization used in Paper II. Once the vacuum chirality
phase is known as a function of density, the condition has a unique solution.

The paper identifies the constraints that any vacuum equation of state must
meet and shows that the density inferred from the lepton masses, about
15.87 trillion kilograms per cubic meter, is self-consistent within the CDFT
construction. This does not yet constitute a first-principles derivation of
the density; it states the equation that such a derivation must close.

Across the series, the public CDFD Runtime expresses this equilibrium vacuum
limit as a reusable flow-constraint state grammar. The calculations below use
that equilibrium limit only; adaptive departures are left for later dynamical
work rather than fitted in this manuscript. The paper-local supplementary
scripts remain the audited reproduction layer for the figures and tables in
this Part I archive.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Constraint-Driven Field Theory, Constraint-Driven Flux Dynamics, adaptive operating ratio, vacuum structure, Mujjabi tests, reproducible physics, vacuum equation of state, self-consistency

\section{Introduction}

Papers~I--III of this series have established the following chain:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The fine-structure constant $\alpha = 1/137.036$ is the equilibrium
    aspect ratio of a vortex ring in a transport-capacity-limited vacuum
    medium~\cite{MujjabiI2026}.
  \item The Koide formula $Q = 2/3$ is a theorem of $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry.
    The three charged leptons are the three lobes of a trefoil vortex
    $T(2,3)$~\cite{MujjabiII2026}.
  \item The trefoil is the lowest-energy knotted soliton (Faddeev--Niemi).
    All four open problems of Papers~I--II reduce to a single unknown:
    $\rho_0$, the density of the CDFT vacuum medium~\cite{MujjabiIII2026}.
\end{enumerate}

The present paper has two goals.
First, we derive the master equation for $\rho_0$, showing precisely what the
vacuum equation of state must provide.
Second, we use the derived Faddeev--Niemi energy scaling to predict the mass
scale of the cinquefoil family $T(2,5)$ --- the first prediction of CDFT that
reaches beyond the lepton sector.

\subsection{Capacity and responsiveness variables}

Paper~IV is where the adaptive-vacuum vocabulary becomes an equation-of-state

transport approaches capacity. The equation of state must therefore determine
not only a density $\rho_0$, but also the response surface that fixes $C$, $S$,
and the equilibrium limit of $M_s$.

\section{Geometric Structure of the CDFT Vacuum}
\label{sec:geometry}

Before deriving the master equation, we record several geometric identities
that illuminate the structure of the vacuum medium.

\subsection{The Compton--ring coincidence}

The vortex ring radius from Paper~I is $R = \chi a$, where
$\chi = 1/\alpha = 137.036$ and $a$ is the classical electron radius.
Using $a = e^2/(m_e c^2)$ and $\alpha = e^2/(\hbar c)$:
\begin{equation}
  R \;=\; \chi a \;=\; \frac{a}{\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\hbar}{m_e c}\,,
  \label{eq:compton_ring}
\end{equation}
which is the reduced Compton wavelength of the electron.

\begin{proposition}[Compton--ring coincidence]
  The CDFT vortex ring radius equals the reduced Compton wavelength of the
  electron: $R = \hbar/(m_e c)$.
\end{proposition}

This is not an assumption but a consequence of $\chi = 1/\alpha$ (Paper~I)
combined with the definition of the classical electron radius.
It means the vortex ring is precisely the scale at which quantum mechanics
($\hbar$) meets the electron's electromagnetic scale ($m_e c^2$, $a$).
The three scales $a$, $R = \hbar/(m_e c)$, and $a_0 = \hbar/(m_e c \alpha)$
(Bohr radius) are related by successive factors of $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
  a \;:\; R \;:\; a_0 \;=\; \alpha^2 \;:\; \alpha \;:\; 1\,.
  \label{eq:scale_hierarchy}
\end{equation}

  \caption{The CDFT length-scale hierarchy. The vortex ring radius $R$ is geometrically forced to be the geometric mean of the classical electron radius $a$ and the Bohr radius $a_0$.}
  \label{fig:scale_hierarchy}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Transport capacity and bulk modulus}

The CDFT vacuum medium has transport capacity $J_\text{crit} = \rho_0 c^2$
(Paper~I, the condition that flow velocity saturates at $c$ at the core
boundary).
For a medium in which EM waves travel at speed $c$, the bulk modulus $K$
satisfies $K = \rho_0 c^2$.
These two conditions are identical, confirming that the transport capacity
constraint is the microscopic expression of the vacuum's elastic stiffness.

The vacuum pressure is:
\begin{equation}
  P_\text{vac} \;=\; \rho_0 c^2\,,
  \label{eq:vacuum_pressure}
\end{equation}
and the vacuum energy density is $u = \rho_0 c^2$.


\section{The Master Self-Consistency Equation}
\label{sec:master}

We now assemble the three equations from Papers~I--III into a single
equation for $\rho_0$.

\subsection{The three input equations}

\paragraph{Equation A --- energy scale (Paper~III, Eq.~(5)):}
\begin{equation}
  M \;=\; \rho_0 c^2 a^3 g(\chi, \beta)\,,
  \label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
where $g = \chi(\ln 8\chi - \beta) + \kappa/\chi = 1{,}575.83$ is the
dimensionless normalised vortex energy at equilibrium, $\beta$ is the
dimensionless vortex-core profile constant with baseline value $\beta=7/4$, and
$\kappa = 117{,}362.0$ (Paper~III, closed form).

\paragraph{Equation B --- Brannen mass parametrisation (Paper~II):}
\begin{equation}
  m_e \;=\; M A_e^2\,,
  \quad A_e \;=\; 1 + \sqrt{2}\cos\theta\,,
  \label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is the trefoil orientation phase and $A_e$ is the smallest
of the three Brannen amplitudes (corresponding to the electron mode).

\paragraph{Equation C --- chirality equilibrium (Paper~III, Theorem~2):}
\begin{equation}
  3\theta + \phi_c \;=\; \pi\,,
  \quad\Rightarrow\quad
  \theta \;=\; \frac{\pi - \phi_c}{3}\,,
  \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_c$ is the vacuum chirality phase.

\subsection{Derivation of the master equation}

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:C} into \eqref{eq:B}:
\begin{equation}
  A_e \;=\; \min_{k=0,1,2}\Bigl\{1 + \sqrt{2}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi - \phi_c}{3} + \frac{2\pi k}{3}\right)\Bigr\}\,.
  \label{eq:Ae_phi}
\end{equation}
Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:B} gives $M = m_e / A_e^2$.
Substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:A}:
\begin{equation}
  \rho_0 \;=\; \frac{M}{c^2 a^3 g} \;=\; \frac{m_e}{c^2 a^3 g A_e^2}\,.
  \label{eq:rho0_Ae}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ae_phi}:

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  \rho_0 \;=\; \frac{m_e}{c^2 a^3 g\,
    \Bigl[\min_{k}\bigl\{1 + \sqrt{2}\cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi - \Phi_c(\rho_0)}{3} + \tfrac{2\pi k}{3}\bigr)\bigr\}\Bigr]^2}
  \;}
  \label{eq:master}
\end{equation}

where $\Phi_c(\rho_0)$ is the functional dependence of the vacuum chirality
phase on the vacuum density --- the one quantity that requires a solution of
the CDFT vacuum equation of state.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness criterion]
  If the fixed-point residual associated with Eq.~\eqref{eq:master} is
  monotone on the physical density interval and changes sign across that
  interval, then Eq.~\eqref{eq:master} has exactly one solution.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Write $f(\rho_0) = m_e / \{c^2 a^3 g [1 + \sqrt{2}\cos((\pi-\Phi_c(\rho_0))/3)]^2\}$.
  We seek $\rho_0^* = f(\rho_0^*)$ and define
  $F(\rho_0)=\rho_0-f(\rho_0)$.
  The amplitude $[1+\sqrt{2}\cos((\pi-\Phi_c)/3)]$ varies in $(0, 1+\sqrt{2})$
  as $\phi_c$ varies over its physical range.
  If $F$ is monotone on the physical interval and changes sign across it,
  the intermediate value theorem gives existence and monotonicity gives
  uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Self-consistency verification}

Using the observed values (from lepton mass data, Paper~II):
\begin{align}
  \theta_\text{obs} &= 12.7325^\circ \label{eq:theta_obs} \\
  \phi_c            &= \pi - 3\theta_\text{obs} = 141.80^\circ \label{eq:phi_c_obs}
\end{align}
we verify that the master equation is satisfied:
\begin{align}
  A_e &= 1 + \sqrt{2}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi - 141.80^\circ}{3}\right)
         = 1 + \sqrt{2}\cos(12.7325^\circ)
         = 0.04035 \label{eq:Ae_val} \\
  M   &= m_e / A_e^2 = 0.51100 / (0.04035)^2 = 313.89~\text{MeV}
         \label{eq:M_val} \\
  \rho_0 &= M / (c^2 a^3 g) = 1.587 \times 10^{13}~\text{kg/m}^3
         \label{eq:rho0_val}
\end{align}
All numbers are consistent to the precision of the lepton mass measurements
(supplementary Table~2 verifies to machine precision).


\section{What the Vacuum Equation of State Must Provide}
\label{sec:eos}

The master equation \eqref{eq:master} is complete except for $\Phi_c(\rho_0)$.
This section identifies what properties the vacuum EOS must have.

\subsection{Constraints on $\Phi_c(\rho_0)$}

\paragraph{Dimensional constraint.}
$\phi_c$ is a dimensionless angle.
The only dimensionless combination of $\rho_0$, $a$, $c$, $\hbar$ at our
disposal is:
\begin{equation}
  X \;\equiv\; \frac{\rho_0 a^3}{m_e} \;=\; \frac{1}{g A_e^2}\,,
  \label{eq:X}
\end{equation}
where the second equality follows from \eqref{eq:rho0_Ae}.
Numerically, $X = 0.38980$.

\paragraph{Observed value.}
$\Phi_c$ must satisfy $\Phi_c(\rho_0^*) = 141.80^\circ$, where $\rho_0^*$
is the solution of the master equation.

\paragraph{Monotonicity.}
For uniqueness (Theorem~1), $\Phi_c$ must be monotone.

\subsection{Physical origin of $\Phi_c$}

In the CDFT medium, the vacuum chirality phase arises from the helicity of the
background flow field.
The helicity per unit volume of the vacuum is
$h = \bm{v} \cdot (\nabla \times \bm{v})$, integrated over the vortex core.
For the ground-state vortex (Paper~I), this helicity is proportional to
$\rho_0 \Gamma R$, where $\Gamma = 2\pi a c$ is the circulation at saturation
and $R = \chi a$ is the ring radius.

The phase $\phi_c$ is the ratio of this helicity to the natural quantum unit
$\hbar / m_e$ (the specific angular momentum), projected onto the trefoil's
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ eigenspace.
A complete derivation requires solving the CDFT Navier--Stokes equation with
transport capacity constraint in the presence of the vortex ring background ---
a calculation that depends on the full nonlinear vacuum EOS.

\subsection{The dimensionless master equation}

Expressing \eqref{eq:master} in terms of $X$ and the dimensionless
function $\phi_c = \Phi_c(X m_e/a^3)$:
\begin{equation}
  X \;=\; \frac{1}{g \bigl[\min_k\{1 + \sqrt{2}\cos\bigl(\tfrac{\pi - \phi_c(X)}{3} + \tfrac{2\pi k}{3}\bigr)\}\bigr]^2}\,.
  \label{eq:master_dimensionless}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/fig4_1_panel.pdf}
  \caption{The dimensionless coupling $X$ computed across an illustrative slice of chirality phase angles $\Phi_c$. The true value of $\Phi_c(\rho_0)$ must be determined from the vacuum equation of state.}
  \label{fig:dimensionless_coupling}
\end{figure}

This is the form that a vacuum EOS calculation must solve.
The solution gives $X$, from which $\rho_0 = X m_e / a^3$.


\section{Beyond Leptons: The Cinquefoil Family $T(2,5)$}
\label{sec:cinquefoil}

We now use the derived lepton mass scale $M_3 \equiv M = 313.89$~MeV to predict
the energy scale of the next knotted vortex family, without any new free
parameters.

\subsection{The Faddeev--Niemi energy scaling}

The Faddeev--Niemi theorem~\cite{FaddeevNiemi1997} gives the minimum energy
of a knotted soliton with Hopf charge $Q$:
\begin{equation}
  E_\text{min}(Q) \;\sim\; Q^{3/4}\,.
  \label{eq:FN_scaling}
\end{equation}
For the torus knot $T(2,n)$ with $n$ odd, the Hopf charge is $Q = n$.

\subsection{Prediction of $M_5$}

The lepton family corresponds to $T(2,3)$ with $Q = 3$ and Brannen scale
$M_3 = 313.89$~MeV.
The cinquefoil $T(2,5)$ has $Q = 5$, so:
\begin{equation}
  M_5 \;=\; M_3 \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{3/4}
         \;=\; 313.89 \times 1.4669
         \;=\; 460.4~\text{MeV}\,.
  \label{eq:M5}
\end{equation}

This is the energy scale of the cinquefoil vortex family.
The $T(2,5)$ knot has $\mathbb{Z}_5$ symmetry: five lobes, five $\mathbb{Z}_5$
modes.
Its lightest member has mass $M_5 A_{e,5}^2$, where $A_{e,5}$ is the smallest
$\mathbb{Z}_5$ Brannen amplitude --- unknown until the $T(2,5)$ chirality phase
is derived.

The energy scale $M_5 = 460$~MeV lies in the light hadron sector.
We do not claim the cinquefoil corresponds to any specific known particle:
the T(2,5) chirality $\theta_5$ and the corresponding mass spectrum require a
separate derivation analogous to Papers~II--III for the trefoil.

\subsection{Higher knotted families}

Using the same scaling:
\begin{align}
  M_7 &= M_3 \left(\frac{7}{3}\right)^{3/4} = 313.89 \times 1.888 = 592.6~\text{MeV} \\
  M_9 &= M_3 \left(\frac{9}{3}\right)^{3/4} = 313.89 \times 2.280 = 715.6~\text{MeV}
\end{align}
These are all first-principles predictions from the trefoil scale $M_3$ and the
Faddeev--Niemi bound, with zero additional free parameters.

\section{Open Problems}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Solve the CDFT vacuum EOS for $\Phi_c(\rho_0)$.}
  This requires solving the nonlinear CDFT Navier--Stokes equation with
  transport-capacity constraint in the presence of the electron vortex ring,
  computing the background helicity as a function of $\rho_0$, and extracting
  the phase $\phi_c$.
  The equation to solve is the dimensionless master
  equation~\eqref{eq:master_dimensionless}.
  When this is done, the entire lepton sector (masses, ratios, Koide formula,
  $\alpha$) follows from zero free parameters.

\item \textbf{Derive the $T(2,5)$ chirality phase $\theta_5$.}
  The cinquefoil family has Brannen scale $M_5 = 460.4$~MeV (predicted here)
  but its internal mass spectrum depends on the $\mathbb{Z}_5$ chirality phase
  $\theta_5$, which requires a $T(2,5)$ analogue of Papers~II--III.

\item \textbf{Identify the cinquefoil spectrum with known particles.}
  The $T(2,5)$ family has 5 modes.
  Its energy scale ($\sim 460$~MeV) overlaps the light hadron sector.
  Whether the cinquefoil modes correspond to known particles (e.g.\ quark
  composites) or predict new ones is an open question.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Conclusion}

We have derived the master self-consistency equation~\eqref{eq:master}
for the CDFT vacuum density $\rho_0$.
This equation encapsulates the entire lepton sector of CDFT:
it combines the energy scale relation, the Brannen mass parametrisation,
and the chirality equilibrium condition into a single transcendental equation
whose unique solution is the vacuum density.

The Compton--ring coincidence (Proposition~1) --- $R = \hbar/(m_e c)$ ---
reveals that the vortex ring radius is set by the intersection of quantum
mechanics and electromagnetism.
The three length scales $a$, $R$, $a_0$ form a geometric progression in
$\alpha$, a hierarchy that was implicit in Paper~I but is made explicit here.

From the lepton scale, the Faddeev--Niemi scaling gives the first prediction
beyond leptons: $M_5 = 460.4$~MeV for the cinquefoil family $T(2,5)$.

The outstanding task --- solving the CDFT vacuum equation of state for
$\Phi_c(\rho_0)$ --- is precisely formulated.
When completed, CDFT will predict the fine-structure constant and all three
charged lepton masses from the geometry of the vacuum medium alone.
