# Mujjabi Laws, Principles, and Tests

This file gives the public naming layer for Part I. The names are strong by
design, but every entry is tied to a mathematical role and a status. A law here
means a law inside the CDFD framework until independent experiment establishes
it physically.

## Mujjabi Falsification Tests

| Test | Prediction | Primary observable | Falsification condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mujjabi Vacuum Hysteresis Test | An intense pump pulse leaves a finite `M_s` residual before relaxation. | Probe-pulse phase or refractive residual decaying with `tau_M`. | No residual after QED, gas, optics, plasma, thermal, and detector effects are bounded. |
| Mujjabi Alpha-Jitter Test | Extreme flux environments perturb the local vortex core and produce `alpha`-sensitive spectral residuals. | Non-QED residuals in high-field/high-Z spectra correlated with `Psi_s`. | Spectra remain fully explained by QED and known nuclear/field corrections. |
| Mujjabi Rotating-Source Test | Rapid angular momentum in dense sources changes a tiny pressure-gradient residual. | Atom-interferometer or torsion residual correlated with rotor angular momentum. | No residual beyond GR, Newtonian gravity, vibration, thermal, electromagnetic, and mechanical systematics. |
| Mujjabi Decoherence-Memory Test | Controlled systems show a residual history-dependent coherence loss after standard environmental channels are modeled. | Preparation-history-dependent phase or visibility decay. | Decoherence is exhausted by standard open-system terms. |
| Mujjabi Transport-Threshold Test | Friction, jamming, plasma, superconducting, and rupture transitions improve when written as adaptive capacity thresholds. | Transition timing, hysteresis loop, or avalanche threshold. | Established domain models predict the same data with equal or better residuals and no CDFD state variable. |

## Vacuum Engineering Program

Vacuum engineering means controlled manipulation of the CDFD state variables,
not a claim of immediate technology.

| Variable | Engineering handle | Observable |
|---|---|---|
| `Phi` or `J` | field intensity, drive, current, shear, angular momentum, density gradient | flux, phase, spectral, or transport response |
| `C` | material boundary, confinement geometry, topology, medium density | threshold shift or capacity saturation |
| `S` | response surface, susceptibility, relaxation channel | altered slope, compliance, or recovery curve |
| `M_s` | prior loading, pulse history, hysteresis protocol | memory residual, lag, or recovery time `tau_M` |

The design target is to steer a system toward, across, or away from
`Psi_s = 1` and measure a discriminating response. A negative result is useful:
it bounds or kills the relevant CDFD claim.
